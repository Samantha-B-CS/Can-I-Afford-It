Frequently Asked Questions

Use the Lazy Ledger to work out all of your outgoings and try to put any remaining money into a high-interest savings account. Also look into Lifetime ISAs, where the government adds a certain amount to your savings.

When buying a house, remember there are many hidden costs, such as solicitors’ fees, stamp duty and surveyors’ fees. Also take ongoing monthly costs into account, such as:

  • Council tax:
    • Check your local council’s website for more information, and find out which council tax band the house is in.
  • Internet
  • Utilities (gas, electricity and water) Yes, water isn’t free!

Stamp Duty is a tax you must pay to the government if you buy a property or land over a certain price threshold. First-time buyers may qualify for a discount:

  • Up to £300,000: 0% (nothing to pay).
  • £300,001 to £500,000: 5% on the portion within this bracket.

Note: If the purchase price exceeds £500,000, you cannot claim first-time buyer relief and must pay the standard rates on the entire amount.

Although it is not essential, having a good credit score can make lenders see you as more trustworthy—that is, more likely to repay what you borrow. A good credit score can also help you get a lower interest rate.
